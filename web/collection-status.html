Scope that matters

  • SR 1 / Wabash: four curved arch barrels with eight independently restrained springing lines. Pressure acts directly on projected barrel footprints. Deck, fill, shared-pier interaction and foundations are excluded.
  • US 421 / Wildcat: main truss, full main-deck footprint including end cantilevers, floor system, rakers, lower bracing and bearings. Approach spans and substructure are excluded. Joints and sections are idealized; no buckling analysis.
  • SR 1 / Six Mile: continuous slab classification and deck envelope come from plans/NBI; slab thickness, exact span split, walls and foundations are hypothetical.
  • Other collection models: source-specific shell/beam layouts with actual support stations, explicit continuous or independent-span assumptions, and simplified section and foundation properties. Prestressed beam models omit prestress in these pressure increments.

Frozen model assumptions

Beechwood Road Bridge

Whole bridge: 1 kPa full-deck increment

  • Connected elastic beam-shell research model. Gross uncracked concrete; no capacity, rating, fatigue or local contact/solid stress claim.
  • Both visual and analytical column center spacing is 22 ft (corrected from 17.5 ft using PDF 38). Analytical superstructure remains straightened; display values map by component ID and local position.
  • Foundation variants fix either estimated tip nodes (unsupported embedment) or rock-line nodes (rigid rock idealization). Neither includes calibrated soil springs, p-y curves, settlement or true rock-socket bond. These are sensitivity scenarios, not bounds.
  • Post-cure end-bent connections are rigid offsets; interior TH1 pads use estimated translational stiffness and free rotational releases. RC diaphragms connect the beam ends; keyway contact and bearing uplift are not nonlinear.
  • Staged case uses the drawing-permitted alternative continuous wet-deck pour on bare girders, then full composite action for barriers and wearing. Default A/B/C sequential pours, hydration age, creep and shrinkage are not simulated.
  • Prestress is applied to the concrete as balanced equivalent tendon forces with a polygonal centroid path; total force is jacking force times a selected retained fraction. No explicit tendon stiffness, transfer length, end-zone bursting or bond model.
  • Stage increments use separate stiffness models, with new concrete stress-free at activation. Cumulative signed beam stresses and reactions are superposed; displayed displacement is accumulated small-displacement response, not measured camber.
  • Wet loads include 8 in deck, 2 in assumed haunch and 15 psf permanent forms. RC diaphragm and substructure weights are included at idealized sections. Barrier quantities use PDF 49. Approach slabs, wingwalls, soil, utilities and small hardware weights are excluded.
  • Grade/crossfall/camber are straightened. Deck extends 1 ft beyond end-bent axes; bearing centers and girder extents follow the drawing. Frame offsets transmit force and moment without stiffness double counting.
  • Shell stress is principal surface stress reconstructed from unsmoothed integration-point resultants. Beam stress is axial plus biaxial nominal flexure; bearing output is mean compression only. These are different measures, never a safety score.
  • Force and moment equilibrium, shell patch/plate and frame benchmarks are checked. Mesh comparison reports each family; localized peaks at rigid connections may not converge and remain flagged. Field validation is absent.
  • Linear bearing springs can carry tension; signed mean pressure is preserved. Tensile increments are not proof of uplift without the existing gravity state. Drilled-shaft concrete uses the pier modulus as an explicit provisional material assignment.

US 421 over South Fork Wildcat Creek

Main truss and complete main deck: full-width pressure increment | APPROACH SPANS EXCLUDED

  • Assumed-input linear elastic MAIN TRUSS AND DECK ONLY. The 125 ft truss and complete main-deck slab are analyzed. The two 31 ft 8.5 in box-beam approaches, their decks, piers, abutments, rails, repairs and connection hardware are excluded and carry no calculated stress.
  • Pressure acts over the complete 126 ft 10.25 in by 29 ft main-deck rectangle (341.768993880 m2). The 11 in start and 11.25 in end slab overhangs are modeled as shell cantilevers beyond the unchanged bearing lines. Stringers stop at the bearing lines. Drain openings are homogenized within the gross loaded rectangle. Half loading means native Y < 0.
  • The original 1939/1940 standard 1532 is absent. Upper-node elevations are scaled visual reconstructions; chord/channel, stringer, raker and lateral-brace sections are explicit hypothetical inputs, not verified fabrication properties.
  • All truss, raker, lateral, floorbeam and stringer joints use an assumed rigid three-dimensional frame idealization, with no pin releases, gusset flexibility, slip or local joint stress. Frame bending stiffness and rakers provide lateral restraint; no overhead or top-plane cross-braces are invented.
  • Source lower lateral crossed braces meet at explicit shared center nodes. Each lower-brace center is assumed rigidly connected to the center stringer/deck; the central single-panel crossing connection is unverified. The two central truss diagonals meet at the M4 node in each plane. Rakers connect the upper joints to floorbeam outriggers with explicit short rigid offsets.
  • The deck and stringers/floorbeams are assumed fully composite by one-level rigid offsets. The source does not establish this full composite action for all retained members.
  • Analytical deck and lower chords are flattened at the source-generation first-bearing datum. A nominal crown/crossfall offset sets floorbeam elevation; roadway grade and crossfall changes are excluded from analysis.
  • Twin-channel chord area and moments are computed by explicit nonoverlapping plates plus optional top cover; torsion uses the open thin-wall strip approximation. Lacing/battens are omitted from section stiffness. No solid bounding-box section is used.
  • The unsymmetric lateral angle is approximated by its centroidal orthogonal Iy/Iz; product-of-inertia coupling and warping are omitted. This is an explicit equivalent frame section, not a certified angle property.
  • Four bilateral bearing springs use assumed stiffness; the expansion end is longitudinally softer. Fixed foundations mean prescribed lower bearing nodes. Elastic foundations add assumed six-degree spring beds at those same nodes; no pier/foundation stresses are claimed.
  • Load is a pressure increment only: no self-weight, prestress, traffic load rating, construction history, cracking, yielding, fatigue, buckling or second-order stability calculation. Local unaveraged shell/joint peaks may not converge.

SR 1 over Wabash River

Assumed arch model: full projected barrel pressure

  • Assumed-input linear elastic arch-barrel pressure increment, not whole-bridge behavior, an as-built analysis, load rating, condition assessment or field validation.
  • All four actual arch-barrel display members are represented by curved ShellMITC4 elements. Deck, sidewalks, spandrel walls, fill, counterforts, piers, abutments, foundations, parapets and utilities are excluded and receive no stress or displacement claim.
  • Four barrels are independently restrained at their eight springing faces. Adjacent barrels are not coupled through a modeled pier. Shared-pier bending, thrust cancellation, foundation settlement and construction joints are outside this model.
  • Uniform pressure acts vertically on the projected 4 x 21.946 m x 14.174 m arch footprints only. Left-half pressure means native Y < 0. Deck overhangs, pier gaps and the extended floor envelope carry no applied load; no redistribution through fill or deck is invented.
  • The quadratic intrados and vertical thickness t=.356+.650*(2r-1)^2 exactly reproduce the current visualization recipe, but that recipe is itself approximate. Original multi-radius arch setting-out, true radial haunch thickness and construction tolerances are unresolved.
  • The shell reference surface is the middle of the displayed vertical ring depth. Each element uses normal thickness equal to vertical depth divided by its surface metric, preserving the local vertical-slice volume to first order. This is an explicit shell idealization, not a surveyed ring profile.
  • Fixed springings clamp all six degrees of freedom along each springing edge. Elastic springings use bilateral six-direction line springs, independently connected to ground. These brackets are illustrative support assumptions, not measured soil/pier behavior.
  • Elastic springing Kz is the editable bearing_kz_MNpm compatibility parameter in MN/m2 of actual skew springing-line length. Kx=.25*Kz, Ky=.125*Kz; all rotational stiffnesses are frozen at 200 MN*m/rad per metre. Tributary line lengths make support stiffness independent of mesh density.
  • Concrete E is editable, default 30 GPa; Poisson ratio is assumed 0.20. Steel E is not applicable and does not enter this concrete-only solve. The springing stiffness control applies only when foundation=elastic.
  • No self-weight, prestress, reinforcement, cracking, creep, shrinkage, geometric nonlinearity, compression-only contact, fill confinement or actual damage is included. All reported fields are incremental linear-elastic shell response.
  • Shell stress is an unaveraged surface principal normal-stress envelope recovered from membrane and bending resultants. It excludes transverse-shear stress, through-thickness normal stress, solid stress tensors and reinforcement stress.
  • Local stresses near fixed edges, free edges and variable-thickness transitions may be mesh sensitive. Passing displacement refinement and equilibrium does not validate those local peaks or the physical support assumptions.
